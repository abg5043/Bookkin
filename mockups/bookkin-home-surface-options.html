In the meantime:

What is being decided

All three open on the recommendation. None opens on the shelf. The Bright Snap visual language is unchanged — this is information architecture only.

Each direction has a different subject: a person, a trip, a book. That is the real choice. Use Day one to see each one with no household evidence, which is where they differ most.

1 · Someone New

The page introduces a person — author or illustrator — with no record in the household, and the books are their work.

for This is the new-creator quota promoted from a hidden rule into the subject of the page. It's aimed exactly at where your own method runs out: you already scan award-winning authors, so the thing you can't produce by hand is a name you don't have.

day one Strongest of the three. "New to this household" is trivially true on day one, so ranking runs on published quality alone and the page is honest and substantive with zero reactions logged.

for A hit yields a backlist — one good introduction is five to ten future recommendations. No book-level design does that.

against All the risk lands on one pick. A bad way-in book can lose a genuinely great creator, so the dismiss has to be "not right now," never a hard negative.

against It structurally prefers creators who have a backlist, which is a quiet pull back toward the established. Many of the best long-tail books are by people with one or two titles.

Open question: what governs cadence — a new introduction only when there's one worth making, and the page says so plainly otherwise?

2 · The Trip

The unit is a library trip, not a recommendation. It opens at the kitchen table, travels into the aisle, and closes out at bedtime.

for The only direction present at the moment delight rate's denominator is actually set. Right now nothing in the product is there when you decide what to physically carry home.

for It puts last trip's books at the top, which is where reread and reaction capture will actually happen. At one trip a fortnight, a page that reliably closes out the previous trip may be worth more than any ranking change.

for The list is stable — it does not reshuffle on open. "Same list as Tuesday" is a trust-building sentence, and it fixes the staleness problem the old Bag couldn't answer.

for For an unfamiliar book, the job stops being "close the sale" and becomes "earn a pickup" — a much easier job, because in the aisle you can open the book.

against Risks drifting into logistics software — a packing list with covers — where quality quietly stops being the subject.

against Close-out may never happen. It has to be inferred from the next open, not depend on a button you remember to press.

3 · Because You Loved (kept)

Anchored on a book that actually worked, with recommendations hanging off it and the reasoning as the organizing structure.

for Leads with the strongest signal available — a comp title from your own house beats any topic. It converts an unknown book into a known quantity, which is the core trust problem.

for Changed since last round: each recommendation now carries its independent judgement — award, imprint, holdings band — underneath the similarity reason. Similarity says like this; provenance says and good. The old version only showed the first.

for The reasons are now specific enough that they couldn't be reproduced in a catalog search box. "Animals + rhyme" could be; "the joke sits with the reader, not the character" could not.

day one Weakest of the three, and deliberately shown refusing to fake it. It needs one genuinely loved book and cannot be filled with popular titles without lying about where the recommendation came from.

against Narrow if the anchor is stale or taste has moved. One anchor per page.

Corrections applied across all three

  • "New author to you" is gone. Bookkin can't know that — it knows there's no record in its own data, against a physical shelf that was never logged. It now says "no record of her in your house."
  • Generic reasons removed. "Animals + rhyme" is reproducible in a catalog search in thirty seconds; a reason like that tells the caregiver the app has no private knowledge.
  • Confidence is stated, not implied. Trust is destroyed by the gap between claimed and actual confidence, not by misses — so the page says which part it's sure about.
  • Dismiss states are contextual. Before reading: save / not interested / already read it. After reading: the four outcome taps. Never all seven at once.

Not represented here

  • The aisle view behind "I'm at the library."
  • Limited-pool results, which are the expected case when retrieval is working — a one-book bag needs a confident presentation, not a three-card grid with two holes.
  • Multi-child and second-caregiver states.

Books shown are real titles. Awards, imprints and holdings figures are plausible placeholders — none is a verified claim yet.
