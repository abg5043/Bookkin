# Screen inventory

The planned V0.1 screen and overlay inventory is:

1. Responsive Bright Snap application shell and outcome-first guidance.
2. Home and next-library-trip entry.
3. Family shelf with title or author search, status filters, and density-aware responsive behavior.
4. Persistent circular quick-action menu.
5. Quick-log sheet with recent-book and shelf-search selection.
6. Add-book modal or bottom sheet.
7. Book detail and append-only history with correction entry.
8. Child context and current-interest editor.
9. Deterministic `What Bookkin is learning` view.
10. Recommendation context and request-reference selection.
11. Normal bag, limited verified pool, and no-candidate results.
12. Official library catalog handoff and first-use library-system selection.
13. Single ISBN scan and result.
14. Batch scan, one-time classification, review, and partial-error recovery.
15. Private product-quality view for household alpha.
16. Loading, empty, success, no-result, limited-evidence, limited-pool, error, permission, external-handoff, and offline-warning states.

Approved historical checkpoints provide family-shelf discovery and append-only reading history. Checkpoint 5A refines only those existing workflows through Bright Snap, searchable collection patterns, and fast Add and Log overlays. It does not add recommendations, camera behavior, or library behavior.

Checkpoint 6 adds only the library adapter and official search contract, not a standalone settings destination. Checkpoint 7A adds family context, preference evidence, taste evidence, and verified candidate sourcing. Checkpoint 7B adds deterministic scoring and composition without a user-facing bag. Checkpoint 8 adds the outcome-first bag and catalog handoff. Checkpoint 9 adds camera and batch ISBN capture. Checkpoint 10A adds the private quality view; it is not a parent-facing engagement dashboard.

After household alpha, Checkpoint 12A may add application identity and controlled household isolation. Checkpoint 12B may optionally add a separate public acquisition surface. Public navigation never exposes household routes, and private child or reading data never appears in public fixtures or analytics payloads.
