# Checkpoint 5A design exploration

This document is historical decision evidence. It records the nine critique-ready concepts and the owner's selected direction. It does not approve application implementation or create an alternate implementation target without another owner decision.

## Current owner direction

Bright Snap is the selected visual language for the revised SDD. The implementation target is a bounded hybrid, not a literal copy of a social-camera product:

- Retain Bright Snap's yellow, ink, white, focus motifs, polish, and inviting energy.
- Borrow editorial cover hierarchy from Story Atlas and Librarian Atlas.
- Borrow large-shelf search, filtering, and density discipline from Card Catalog.
- Borrow the outcome-first product thesis from Personal Librarian.
- Concentrate energy in capture, confirmation, and recommendation reveal.
- Keep shelf, history, quick-log, profile, modal, and bedtime surfaces calmer.
- Exclude social graphs, public sharing, streaks, popularity, disappearing data, engagement pressure, and invented AI behavior.

Checkpoint 5A still requires a new interactive review of the bounded hybrid before implementation.

## Historical baseline

The pre-exploration baseline was the readable warm action-menu concept represented by `bookkin-readable-action-review.html` in the saved interactive artifacts:

- Warm neutral surfaces and deep plum text.
- Restrained coral, apricot, blush, and lilac accents.
- Large editorial typography and prominent covers.
- One lower-right floating action expanding to explicit `Log a read` and `Add a book` labels.
- Desktop modal and mobile bottom sheet.
- Search by title or author and filters for All, Owned, Borrowed, and Wishlist.
- Tappable cards without repeated `Log reading` links.
- Review comments outside the product UI and no unexplained numbered markers.

This baseline is archived evidence, not a competing implementation target.

## Exploration brief

The concepts were required to differ in composition and visual language, not only palette. Every direction balanced:

1. Fun and warmth for children without becoming babyish.
2. Elegance, simplicity, accessibility, and trust for caregivers.
3. Broad commercial polish and an inviting first impression.
4. Extremely fast mobile access to Log a read and Add a book.
5. A visible connection between reading feedback, changing interests, and better recommendations.

Each concept received product-management and family-UX/accessibility critique for usability, recommendation-value clarity, retention and conversion potential, accessibility, privacy, honest claims, and implementation feasibility.

## Concepts reviewed

Current-shell visual directions:

1. **Story Atlas** - warm, cover-led editorial discovery.
2. **Card Catalog** - compact large-shelf efficiency.
3. **Night Garden** - optional reading-hour atmosphere with a daytime default.
4. **Pop-up Studio** - bold contemporary children's-publishing energy.
5. **Story Thread** - family reading memory as a supporting layer.

Strategic directions:

6. **Personal Librarian** - pure outcome-first recommendation north star.
7. **Librarian Atlas** - Personal Librarian plus editorial cover presence.
8. **Librarian Pop** - Personal Librarian plus commercial energy and scalable shelf mechanics.
9. **Bright Snap** - private camera-native aesthetic inspiration without social, streak, popularity, sharing, or disappearing-data behavior.

Passing critique meant only that an artifact was ready for human review. It did not approve a concept, authorize implementation, or move recommendation, interest, library, or camera work into Checkpoint 5A.

## Product and design hypotheses retained

- **Outcome-first personal librarian:** make better next-library-trip choices more prominent than shelf administration.
- **Visible recommendation evidence:** explain child evidence, caregiver evidence, current interests, and lower-weight historical interests without collapsing provenance.
- **Outcome-based retention:** measure library-trip and reading outcomes instead of time spent, streaks, or app opens.
- **Recommendation roles:** consider closest fit, adjacent discovery, and earlier-interest revisit as explainable composition roles.
- **Optional reading-hour appearance:** test a lower-luminance bedtime treatment without making the product dark-only.
- **Large-shelf list mode:** complement cover-led browsing while retaining a visible reading signal and child appeal.
- **Recent-memory trust layer:** show why suggestions change without creating an engagement feed.
- **Child-assisted choosing:** research a private, low-pressure cover-choice interaction without rewards or public profiles.
- **Caregiver terminology:** use inclusive caregiver language while preserving evidence provenance.

These remain hypotheses unless their owning checkpoint and the human product owner approve them.

## Ideas rejected

- Preselected reactions, event types, books, or shelf statuses.
- Aggregated claims such as `both liked it` that erase provenance.
- Calling an earlier interest a favorite without evidence.
- Hiding current or historical recommendation evidence on phone.
- Universal bedtime, nursery, flower, or dark-only framing.
- Arbitrary prominence that implies a permanent favorite or ranking.
- Dead controls or generic success messages that imply completed work.
- Guarantees that one event will improve recommendations.
- Streaks, guilt, scarcity, unread pressure, and other time-spent mechanics.

## Review findings carried forward

The critique loops exposed misleading defaults, reaction-source collapse, false attribution, unreachable quick actions, modal focus failures, offline contradictions, dead prototype controls, low contrast, undersized touch targets, arbitrary search success, cover-state recovery problems, and incomplete limited-evidence states.

Camera ISBN scanning remains owned by Checkpoint 9. Recommendation and marketing concepts cannot expose child data, use dark patterns, or imply capabilities that do not exist.
